# Screenplay

Second stage. Reads `brief.md`, produces `screenplay.md`.

This is the last stage where changing something is cheap. Every stage after it
multiplies: a scene becomes shots, a shot becomes prompts, a prompt becomes
generated bytes that cost money. So the screenplay is where the person is
asked to look.

## Stop here, once

When the screenplay is written, **ask before going on to shots**. Not "is this
good?" — a question nobody can answer usefully — but the one thing that is
expensive to get wrong:

> The screenplay is ready. Shall I break it into shots, or do you want to
> change something first?

This is the second of the two planned stops (the first is the budget, which
the tool raises once models are priced). Everything between them runs on its
own.

## The continuity bible is not decoration

The stages after this one run separately and cannot see each other. What holds
a character's face the same across twelve generated shots is not memory: it is
that both the character sheet and every shot prompt were written from the same
paragraph. That paragraph is the bible, and it lives at the foot of this file.

## The shape

```markdown
# <title>

## Scene 1 — <slug: INT./EXT. PLACE — TIME>

<Action, present tense, only what a camera can see.>

ALICE
<dialogue>

BOB
<dialogue>

<Action.>

## Scene 2 — …

---

## Continuity

### Characters
**Alice** — <age, build, hair, clothing that does not change, one distinctive
thing>. Speech: <short and clipped | formal | hesitant>.

### Places
**The bus shelter** — <One paragraph a camera could be pointed at: what is in
it, what it is made of, what the light is. Present tense, concrete, no rule
about when we return and no word about what it means.>

<Then one sentence per THING in it that a shot might hold on its own — the
bench, the timetable panel, the broken light. Each standing alone, the way a
character sheet's parts do, because a shot of a place quotes one of them.>

> The timetable panel is behind cracked perspex, one corner lifted, with a
> water stain across the lower third.

### Rules
<Anything that must stay true: a season, a phone that is always broken, a
character who is never seen standing up.>
```

Rules that matter more than the format:

- **Action is what a camera can see.** "She realises he lied" is not action.
  "She stops reading. She does not look up." is.
- **Every scene changes something.** If the situation at the end of a scene is
  the situation at the start, cut the scene, however good the dialogue is.
- **Open in the middle.** A short has no room for a runway; the first line of
  scene one should be answerable only by watching.
- **Dialogue carries under-length.** People interrupt, answer a different
  question, and stop before the end of the thought. Lines that state the theme
  are the first thing to cut.
- **Name every character on first use and keep the name stable.** The
  character sheets and the shot list key off these names, exactly as written.
- **The bible describes appearance and speech, not feelings.** It exists to be
  copied into an image prompt.
- **A place's paragraph and its rules are two different things, and they live
  under two different headings.** *"A strip light, one tube dead, over a
  concrete floor with a drag mark through the dust"* is a paragraph: a camera
  can be pointed at it. *"Whenever we return it is the same hour of the night
  and the same one light"* is a rule: it is about continuity across scenes, and
  it belongs under **Rules**. Pasted into a first frame it would put an
  instruction about future scenes into a still, and the model would try to obey
  it.

## Then

```
emit_artifact(path="screenplay.md", type="markdown",
              facts=["<n> scenes", "<n> characters", "<n>s target"],
              from=["brief.md"])
```

Then re-plan, now that the names exist:

```
plan_artifacts(nodes=[
  {"path": "brief.md",             "type": "markdown", "from": []},
  {"path": "screenplay.md",        "type": "markdown", "from": ["brief.md"]},
  {"path": "characters/alice.md",  "type": "markdown", "from": ["screenplay.md"]},
  {"path": "characters/bob.md",    "type": "markdown", "from": ["screenplay.md"]},
  {"path": "shots.json",           "type": "table",    "from": ["screenplay.md"]},
  {"path": "prompts.md",           "type": "markdown", "from": ["shots.json", "characters/alice.md", "characters/bob.md"]}
])
```

One node per character actually named, at the path the `characters` skill will
use — lower-case, the character's own name, one word. A plan that names a path
no stage produces is a promise the canvas will show as unkept.

**`prompts.md` descends from the sheets as well as from the shots**, and this
is the plan that has to say so. `shot-prompts` pastes those sheets' words into
the prompts and emits with all of them in its `from`, because a person who
edits a sheet has made the prompts stale and the graph is what tells them. A
plan drawing only the shot-list edge draws a graph the last stage will
contradict, and leaves the sheets looking like a dead end until the moment they
are not.
