# Second Key

**Premise.** A night-shift locksmith is called out to open a stranger's door
and finds her own key already in the lock.

- **Genre.** Drama — short serial, mystery.
- **Tone.** Cold, procedural, airless.
- **Length.** 60 seconds.
- **Format.** vertical 9:16
- **Ending.** hooks the next episode

## Protagonist

**Ro Mercer**, thirty-eight, a locksmith who works the night list because it
pays double and nobody talks to her. She wants to finish the call-out and be
home before the buses start. She is afraid that she is losing time — that
there are hours she cannot account for and has stopped counting. The one thing
a camera can see: before she touches any lock, she checks her own pockets,
every time, the way other people check a stove.

## Conflict

The key in the door of flat 11B carries her shop's orange tag and her own
handwriting. She has no memory of cutting it, and the customer who called the
job in does not answer their phone. She cannot walk away: the key is evidence
with her name on it, hanging in a stranger's door at three in the morning, and
leaving it there is worse than turning it.

## The world

A residential tower, eleventh floor, three in the morning, late autumn. One
working strip light on the landing and a second that ticks. Orange paper key
tags on a steel ring. A canvas tool roll with a pick gun in it. A white van
with a magnetic sign on the door, parked across two bays.

## How this brief was decided

The `brief` skill asks three questions in one message and proposes the rest.
The three were fixed in advance for this sample and are recorded here rather
than asked:

- **How long.** 60–90 seconds.
- **Where it plays.** Vertical.
- **What it is for.** An episode that hooks the next one.

Genre, tone, protagonist and world were proposed here, not asked, as the skill
requires. The `drama` skill then took the length to **the lower end of what was
said** — 60 seconds, not 90 — because drama runs long in the writing and is cut
in the edit.

**The plan drawn at this stage is incomplete in one edge, on purpose.** The
skill says so itself: `prompts.md` descends from the character sheets as well
as from the shot list, and the sheets cannot be named before anyone has a name.
`screenplay` completes it. Nothing calls a tool in this sample; both plans are
in the record at `docs/records/kiso-film-sample-2.md`.
