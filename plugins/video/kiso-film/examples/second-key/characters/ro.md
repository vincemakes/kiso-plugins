# Ro Mercer

## Appearance

Ro Mercer is a woman of about thirty-eight, medium height and squarely built,
with straight dark-brown hair cut to the jaw and pushed behind one ear. She has
a broad forehead, heavy eyebrows, and a small white scar through the left
eyebrow. She wears a navy work jacket with a horizontal reflective strip across
the back and a locksmith's shop name stitched in white thread on the left
chest, a plain grey T-shirt beneath it, and dark canvas work trousers with a
flat steel tape measure clipped at the right hip. A carabiner on her belt loop
carries a ring of about a dozen keys with small orange paper tags.

Her hands are broad and short-nailed, with a dark line of graphite under the
right thumbnail.

Her navy work jacket has a horizontal reflective strip across the back and a
locksmith's shop name stitched in white thread on the left chest.

The keys on her belt loop hang from a steel carabiner, about a dozen of them,
each with a small orange paper tag.

## Wardrobe

- **Default.** The navy work jacket, grey T-shirt, dark canvas trousers, tape
  measure at the hip, carabiner of tagged keys at the belt loop. It does not
  change in this episode.
- **Scene 4.** The jacket is unzipped; she has carried the tool roll under one
  arm since Scene 1 and sets it down inside the door.

## Performance

- **Speech.** Short and clipped. Two words where four would do. Her verbal
  habit is answering a different question from the one she was asked.
- **Body.** She stands square, weight even, hands at her sides rather than in
  her pockets. Before she touches any lock she pats down her own jacket
  pockets, left then right, without looking at them.
- **What the camera should catch.** The half-second where she decides not to do
  something — the hand that starts toward the key and stops.

## Continuity

- The pocket check before every lock. It is the first thing her hands do.
- Graphite under the right thumbnail: she writes the tags in pencil.
- The scar through the left eyebrow.
- The carabiner of orange-tagged keys is on her belt loop in every scene.

---

**One sentence per part a camera can isolate.** After the paragraph, three
sentences: the hands, the jacket, the keys. Five of this episode's shots are
inserts that hold one of those and nothing else, and each quotes exactly the
sentence for the part in frame rather than the whole paragraph. The repetition
between the paragraph and the sentences is the point — the paragraph is for a
shot that shows her, the sentences are for a shot that shows a part of her.

**Chosen here, not in the screenplay.** The graphite under the thumbnail, the
tape measure at the hip, and the count of about a dozen keys on the carabiner.
The screenplay establishes the tags, the pencil and the jacket; these three are
mine, and a person can strike them without touching the bible.
